Live positions

Each bet is NO on "will the price be above the strike". It pays $1 per contract if AAA's posted average is at or below the strike. Paid is the price per contract; now worth is what selling it right now would bring. Price move since entry is how many cents the market has moved our way (+) or against us (−); after settlement it uses the last price before trading closed, the "closing line." Review grades each bet once it settles. Times are Mountain time.

Long-shot fades by category

Same rules in every category: the bot takes every market that qualifies, so the counts differ only because Kalshi lists more of some kinds. What makes the comparison fair is enough independent events per category. Events are distinct settlement days or games; several strikes on the same day count once. Enough data = 300+ events, Getting there = 100+, Too early = under 100. The bot keeps paper-testing every category automatically; Enough data by is the date each one should reach 300 at its pace over the last two weeks. Gas & diesel is split out of Economics because it is what the live bot trades.

News desk

Practice only, new Nov 5

A research scan reads the news a few times a day and names specific Kalshi markets it thinks the news has mispriced: economy, politics, energy, crypto, world events. The bot paper-buys each idea only at or below the idea's price limit, and every call is graded when the market settles. It trades for real only if it beats the market over a few hundred ideas.

Entry filters: would skipping these bets have helped?

Each rule is replayed on every settled paper gas bet it has data for. A rule helps only if the bets it skips lost money overall. Skipping long shots that would have won costs profit, so a rule must catch more losses than it gives up. Wholesale rules use what was known the day before entry. A rule needs a few hundred bets and a clear gain before it goes near real money.

How much can the gas markets hold?

Each time a gas market qualifies, the bot reads Kalshi's order book: how many contracts are for sale at our price, and within 2 cents of it. Summed per day, this is roughly the most money the gas strategy could put to work. It caps how big this strategy can ever get.

Exit rules: sell early or hold to the end?

Uses Kalshi's hourly price history for each paper bet. Selling means selling the NO contract at the market price, paying the fee again. Losers cut are bets that would have lost anyway; winners sold early are the cost of the rule. Builds up a little each run as price history is downloaded.

News risk flags

A morning research scan for things that push gas prices up: Gulf hurricanes, refinery or pipeline outages, state gas-tax changes, crude oil spikes. Paper only: flags are graded against what happened before they are allowed to block a real bet.
